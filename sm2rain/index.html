SM2RAIN: rainfall estimated from soil moisture

The soil works as a natural rain gauge. When it rains, the soil gets wetter; SM2RAIN reads those increases in satellite soil moisture and inverts the soil water balance to tell how much rain fell.

Conceptual sketch. Satellite rainfall products usually look at clouds from the top down; SM2RAIN works from the bottom up, starting from the ground, which makes it a fully independent estimate that can be merged with other products.

Going operational. SM2RAIN-ASCAT global rainfall will be released operationally by EUMETSAT H SAF, with about one week of delay. A bottom-up product will then be updated routinely, alongside the top-down ones.

Independent tests, 2026. In the largest global benchmark to date, 24 precipitation datasets driving a hydrological model over 18,428 catchments, GPM+SM2RAIN stands out among the purely satellite-based products (Abbas et al., 2026, HESS).

New in 2025: SM2RAIN-Dual. A global rainfall product obtained by applying SM2RAIN to both SMAP and ASCAT soil moisture, then fusing the two estimates. Daily, 10 km, 2015 to 2022, openly available on Zenodo.

From a catchment to an operational service

  1. 2013

    The algorithm is published in Geophysical Research Letters, tested with in-situ soil moisture.

  2. 2014

    First global application to satellite soil moisture (ASCAT, AMSR-E, SMOS) in JGR Atmospheres.

  3. 2018–2023

    Open global products: SM2RAIN-CCI, SM2RAIN-ASCAT, GPM+SM2RAIN and the 24-year SM2RAIN-Climate record.

  4. 2025

    SM2RAIN-Dual fuses SMAP and ASCAT into a 10 km daily global product.

  5. 2026

    Release planned at EUMETSAT H SAF. A global benchmark places GPM+SM2RAIN among the best satellite-only products, and other groups extend the method, including versions that need no rain gauges for calibration.

Run the algorithm yourself

Code

SM2RAIN is open source in Python, MATLAB and R. A reader extracts SM2RAIN-ASCAT time series at one or more locations.

Method

The algorithm is described in Brocca et al. (2013) and its global application in Brocca et al. (2014). For the story behind it, read a short history of SM2RAIN.

Where SM2RAIN is used

Floods and landslides

SM2RAIN rainfall improves flood simulations, from single catchments (Massari et al., 2014) to 975 basins across Europe, and feeds geo-hydrological risk assessment with the Civil Protection.

Better merged products

Combined with GPM, SM2RAIN improves daily rainfall over Italy (Ciabatta et al., 2015) and in data-scarce regions where rain gauges are rare.

Climate and drought

SM2RAIN-Climate offers a long, independent record for trend and drought analysis (Mosaffa et al., 2023, Scientific Data).

A growing bottom-up community

Other groups now build on SM2RAIN. SM2RAIN-NWF couples it with a net water flux model and is moving towards self-calibration, so rainfall can be estimated where no rain gauges exist (Saeedi et al., 2026, npj Clim. Atmos. Sci.). Independent studies also compare bottom-up and top-down products head to head.

Independent benchmarks

Tested on 18,428 catchments worldwide against 23 other datasets, GPM+SM2RAIN performs strongly among satellite-only rainfall (Abbas et al., 2026).

Irrigation

Applied where farmers irrigate, the same idea reveals water that did not come from rain. This is the basis of our irrigation work.
