How much water is used for irrigation?

Irrigation takes more than 70% of the water we withdraw from rivers and aquifers, yet it is barely monitored anywhere. We measure it from space, using satellite soil moisture.

Conceptual sketch. Soil gets wetter after rain, but over irrigated fields it also gets wetter on dry days. By inverting the soil water balance (the SM-based inversion, born from SM2RAIN) we turn those extra increases into irrigation amounts and timing.

Now available: irrigation water use over all of Europe at 1 km. IWU-SMinv, from our ESA Irrigation-EU project, maps irrigation every 14 days from Copernicus Sentinel-1 soil moisture. Version 1.0 covers January 2016 to September 2025 and can be updated operationally; version 1.1, which uses GLEAM evaporation, covers 2016–2024 and generally matches ground data more closely. Download it from Zenodo, read the preprint, or run the processing code.

Satellites or global models? New in Earth’s Future. We compared satellite irrigation estimates with five global hydrological models over four hotspots: CONUS, the Ebro basin, India and the Murray–Darling basin. Where the two disagree most, the satellite estimates come closer to reported irrigation volumes (Dari et al., accepted; preprint).

Choose the right soil moisture product. Model-based SMAP Level 4 soil moisture barely responds to irrigation and should not be used to detect it; observation-driven products are needed to see human water use.

From one idea to a European product

  1. 2018

    First method, using coarse resolution satellite soil moisture, published in JAG and featured by ESA. The idea “SPACE-IRR: How much water for irrigation?” won the BayWa Smart Farming Challenge at the Copernicus Masters.

  2. 2020–2023

    ESA Irrigation+ (PI). The first regional, high-resolution irrigation datasets from space: 1 km over the Ebro and Po basins with Sentinel-1, 6 km over the Murray–Darling basin with CYGNSS (ESA story).

  3. 2023–2025

    ESA Climate Change Initiative, Anthropogenic Water Use (PI). Long-term irrigation records from several EO methods over major irrigated regions, now published in ESSD.

  4. 2026

    Satellite and model estimates compared head to head over four irrigation hotspots, accepted in Earth’s Future.

  5. 2025–2027

    Irrigation-EU (ESA, PI): the first European-scale irrigation dataset at 1 km, released in 2026 as IWU-SMinv. CCI-AWU IMPRES (ESA, co-PI, led by Jacopo Dari, University of Perugia): more than 20 years of irrigation water use at 0.1° over CONUS, Europe and Australia, from several methods.

Help us validate

Satellite estimates need ground truth, and farm-level irrigation records are scarce. We are part of a community effort to build the first open database of in-situ irrigation observations. If you hold irrigation volume or timing data, get in touch.
